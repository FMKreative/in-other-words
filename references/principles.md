# Principles and sources

In Other Words draws on three main writing references. Their practical guidance is incorporated in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md); this page explains the sources behind it.

## George Orwell

[Politics and the English Language](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/) informs the emphasis on concrete meaning, familiar words, direct verbs, and removing stale phrases and needless words. Its allowance for breaking a rule supports using judgment when a mechanical edit would make the writing worse.

## The Kansas City Star

[The Star Copy Style](https://jollycontrarian.com/images/2/26/Kansas_Star_style_guide.pdf) informs direct openings, focused sentences, exact wording, and restraint with modifiers. These are the newspaper's instructions; they should not be attributed to Hemingway as their author. The skill adapts the writing advice without adopting the period's house style.

## Digital.gov

The [plain language guides](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/) inform the emphasis on the intended reader: organize around what they need, make the main point easy to find, and clarify actions and responsibility.

## A specific influence from ASD-STE100

The project credited [ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English](https://www.asd-ste100.org/) for one editing principle: recast a sentence when replacing individual words would sound unnatural or change its meaning. In Other Words retains that principle. It does not implement the standard or claim compliance with it.

## Applying the principles

Preserve meaning, necessary nuance, and the writer's voice. Use these English-language references as guidance for clarity while following the language being written, including its idiom, register, and punctuation.
